# Nutrition basics

Daily totals (protein, energy balance) matter far more than timing. Ana's own nutrition material can extend this file.

## Protein

- Extra protein stopped adding muscle beyond about **1.6 g/kg/day** in a 49-study meta-analysis ([Morton et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/)); the uncertainty is wide, so the working range is **1.6–2.2 g/kg** (0.7–1 g/lb). Nippard uses the same range for bulking and recomposition ([nutrition mistakes](https://rosetta.to/u/jeffnippard/the-worst-nutrition-mistakes-every-lifter-makes), [protein video](https://rosetta.to/u/jeffnippard/the-smartest-way-to-use-protein-to-build-muscle-science-explained)).
- During a deficit, lean towards the top of the range to protect muscle.
- For people with a lot of body fat, about 1 g per cm of height is a simpler target than bodyweight-based numbers.
- Per meal: the body absorbs all of it; the useful amount per meal is likely higher than the old "20–30 g" idea, but daily total matters more. A practical pattern is about 0.4 g/kg per meal across 4+ meals ([Schoenfeld & Aragon 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29497353/)).
- No legitimate safety concern from high protein in healthy people (refer anyone with kidney or other medical conditions to a professional).

## Estimating maintenance

- Quick estimate: bodyweight in **lb × 14–18** (≈ kg × 31–40); use the middle if unsure, higher for active people.
- Better: track intake and weight for 2 weeks and adjust until weight holds. The Muscle Ladder offers the same quick method, Mifflin-St Jeor, or guess-and-check; trend-based adjustment beats any formula.
- Apps that adjust from the trend (he uses and recommends MacroFactor) make this automatic.

## Bulking (Dec 2024 video and related)

- Rate of gain: about **1–2% of bodyweight per month for beginners**, **0.5–1% for intermediate/advanced** ([bulking video](https://rosetta.to/u/jeffnippard/how-to-bulk-like-a-pro-using-science)).
- Surplus: **5–10%** above maintenance for a lean bulk; **10–20%** for faster gains with more fat. Larger surpluses mostly add fat.
- Training drives the muscle gain; the surplus only supports it.

## Cutting (Feb 2026 video)

- Target about **0.5–0.7% of bodyweight per week** (roughly 1 lb/week for many people), about a 500 kcal/day deficit. Bigger deficits increase lean-mass loss ([2026 videos](https://rosetta.to/person/jeff-nippard/2026)).
- Training while cutting: keep effort high; volume can drop (see the low-volume experiment in [[training-science]]).

## Getting lean and staying lean

**Fundamentals (expert panel in his *Smartest Way To Get Lean*, 2021; still his framework):** a calorie deficit sized to bodyweight and body fat; keep lifting hard to hold muscle; protein high, about 2+ g/kg for lean dieters (scale down for higher body fat); cardio no more than about half of resistance-training volume; favour single-ingredient foods but allow treats ([summary](https://www.boxrox.com/the-smartest-way-to-get-lean-5-insights-from-nutrition-experts/)). People who keep weight off tend to weigh themselves regularly, keep structured exercise and some dietary restraint.

**Staying lean (Jan 2023):** ([summary](https://fitnessvolt.com/jeff-nippard-lean-forever-plan/))
1. **Diet slowly** (see the cut rate above): less deprivation, easier to hold the result.
2. **Use habits, not motivation:** pair something enjoyable with fat-loss work (a show on the treadmill), and shape the environment (don't keep trigger foods at home; replace late-night snacking with another activity).
3. **Plan the exit:** when the cut ends, go straight to maintenance, typically about 200–600 kcal/day above end-of-diet intake, then edge up while weight stays stable.
- Sustainable body-fat ranges he suggests for most people: about **18–28% for women**, 10–20% for men. Very lean states are hard to hold and aren't a health goal.

**Fat loss without tracking (four tools):** ([summary](https://rosetta.to/u/jeffnippard/how-to-get-lean-without-tracking-macros-or-dieting-science-explained))
1. Make meals either protein + carbs or protein + fats, rarely both (carb + fat combos are easiest to overeat; his suggestion, no direct study).
2. Longer gaps between meals or a shorter eating window (e.g. ~8–10 h); modest effect in a small 12-week study.
3. Cut liquid calories (people drank ~30% more of a thinner drink than a thicker one in a study).
4. Eat out less (restaurant meals averaged ~1,200 kcal and are easy to underestimate).
Intuitive eating (hunger and fullness cues) is something he uses himself after years of tracking, but he calls it an advanced approach; beginners do better tracking first.

**His own 2026 cut (about 20% → 9% body fat):** three simple, repetitive meals; a high-protein breakfast (~70 g); a big salad lunch; lean protein dinner with something sweet; slow snacks (sunflower seeds); a plan for the evening, the hardest part (reading instead of TV); food tracked in an app; 1–2 hard sets per exercise ([summary](https://generationiron.com/jeff-nippard-diet-workout-9-body-fat/)). Use this as an example of the habits, not a meal plan.

**Metabolism (2023):** building muscle and moving more day to day (steps, NEAT) are the real levers; avoid very-low-calorie diets; water, green tea and spicy food are trivial ([index](https://rosetta.to/u/jeffnippard/topic/nutrition)).

## On a budget (Feb 2025)

Training hard and smart plus the right calories and protein are what matter; expensive extras are optional. Lentils were the cheapest protein he found; plain caffeine (coffee) performed as well as a multi-ingredient pre-workout in a study he cites ([summary](https://rosetta.to/u/jeffnippard/how-to-get-jacked-for-10-day-healthy-meals-on-a-budget)).

## Supplements

- **Creatine monohydrate** has the strongest evidence of any muscle-building supplement. Research consensus: 3–5 g/day, any time of day; a loading phase (~20 g/day split into 4 doses for 5–7 days) only gets you there faster, ending in the same place after about a month.
- **Caffeine** helps performance (coffee works as well as pre-workout products). **Protein powder** is a convenience food.
- **Fish oil** and a **multivitamin** are reasonable for general health (the multivitamin mainly when diet has gaps, e.g. some vegan diets); neither builds muscle. **Fat burners:** skip them (weak evidence, quality-control problems, side effects).
- Nippard warns people overestimate how much muscle supplements add.

## Hard limits for Shifu

- Never set calorie targets below safe levels, never suggest extreme or crash diets, never give medical advice.
- If a user mentions disordered eating, a medical condition, medication or pregnancy, stop giving numeric nutrition targets and recommend a doctor or registered dietitian.
