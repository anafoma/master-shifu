# Programme design patterns (Nippard's paid programmes, high level)

General methods seen in *The Pure Bodybuilding Program*, described in Shifu's own words. The programme's workouts, exercise lists, sets, reps and weekly progressions are deliberately **not** included: they're the paid product. Anyone who wants them should buy the programme and build it in their own Hevy, where Shifu can coach from the logs.

## Structure

- **10 weeks in two blocks of 5.** Block 1 is a build phase. A **semi-deload week** at the transition keeps sessions but avoids failure and goes lighter. Block 2 is a "novelty" phase that changes exercises to give a fresh stimulus.
- Shifu applies the same shape when planning a 10-week block: about 5 weeks of building, a lighter week, then about 5 weeks with rotated exercise variations for the same muscles (see variation rules in [[muscle-ladder]]).

## Effort structure: early sets vs last set

- Working sets are split into **early sets** (slightly easier) and a **last set** (pushed to failure on most exercises, not all).
- This is a high-effort programme: most working sets land at about RPE 9–10. That is harder than the Muscle Ladder's general default for heavy compounds (RPE 6–8). Shifu's reconciliation: use the higher-effort style for machines, cables and isolation work, and keep heavy free-weight compounds a little further from failure unless the user is experienced and wants a high-effort block.
- A **last-set intensity technique** (e.g. lengthened partials, drop set, myo-reps) is used on selected exercises only, never everywhere. See rung 9 in [[muscle-ladder]].

## Warm-up by number of warm-up sets

General warm-up of about 5–10 min (light cardio plus a few dynamic arm and leg swings), then for the first exercises:

| Warm-up sets | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | One set at about 60% of working weight, 6–10 reps |
| 2 | ~50% for 6–10, then ~70% for 4–6 |
| 3 | ~45% for 6–10, ~65% for 4–6, ~85% for 3–4 |

Later exercises for already-warm muscles need 0–1 warm-up sets.

## Weak-point day

- One session a week is reserved for **arms plus 1–2 exercises for a chosen weak point.** Pick one weak point per block, not several.
- Not every weak point needs more sets:
  - **Hamstrings, mid-back, biceps, triceps:** usually already get plenty of direct or indirect work. Improve execution and effort before adding sets; extra sets risk "junk volume".
  - **Calves, upper traps, abs:** fix execution first (calves: pause in the bottom stretch; traps: squeeze at the top; abs: let the lower back round as you crunch), then add 1–2 sets per exercise at most if it's a real priority. For visible abs, body fat (diet) matters more than ab volume.
  - **Shoulders, lats, quads, glutes, chest, neck:** good candidates for 1–2 extra exercises on the weak-point day.
- This matches the Muscle Ladder's caution about hamstring volume (recovery limits around 12–15 sets/week).

Every exercise has a demo and approved substitutions. Shifu does the same by offering like-for-like swaps from [[exercise-tiers]] rather than ad-hoc replacements.
