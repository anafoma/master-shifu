# Training science

Weekly volume and effort are the biggest levers for growth. Frequency, rest and range of motion are mostly ways to deliver them. The "Shifu default" column is a starting point built from the research, not a number the studies prescribe.

| Variable | What the research shows | Shifu default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weekly sets per muscle | Growth keeps rising with more sets, with diminishing returns; strength plateaus sooner. About 4 weekly sets already beats the smallest detectable effect ([Pelland et al.](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460/version/587), 67 studies) | Priority muscles 12–20 sets/week; others 6–10; maintenance about 4 |
| Counting sets | Counting an indirect set as half a set ("fractional") predicted results best | Bench set = 1 chest + 0.5 triceps + 0.5 front delt |
| Frequency | At equal weekly volume, frequency adds little for size but helps strength | Each muscle at least 2×/week, mainly to spread volume |
| Sets per session | More sets per session still help, with diminishing returns ([Remmert et al. 2025](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537)) | Cap about 6–8 hard sets per muscle per session; add a day rather than more sets |
| Proximity to failure | Growth increases as sets end closer to failure; strength barely depends on it ([Robinson et al. 2024](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4089104?lng=en)). Failure vs 1–2 RIR gave similar growth in trained lifters but more fatigue | Isolation: last set to failure. Compounds: 1–2 RIR |
| Stretch / ROM | Strong benefit for seated leg curls and calves. In trained lifters' arms, lengthened partials matched full reps ([Wolf, Nippard et al. 2025](https://peerj.com/articles/18904)) | Full ROM with a deep stretch; lengthened partials optional, default for calves |
| Rest | Small benefit to resting over 60 s; nothing extra detected beyond 90 s ([Singer et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39205815/)) | Isolation 1.5–2 min; heavy compounds 2–3 min |
| Deloads | A week off midway through 9 weeks didn't change growth and slightly reduced strength gains ([Coleman et al. 2024](https://peerj.com/articles/16777)) | Deload when performance stalls or joints ache, not on a fixed calendar |

## Failure: how Nippard applies it (2024)

- In his failure videos he concludes that training to failure costs more recovery than it adds growth, so use it sparingly. A study he covers found jump and squat performance impaired for about 24 h and bench for 48 h after failure training.
- His rule of thumb: save failure for **isolation exercises**, on the **last set of the last exercise** for a muscle.
- Sources: [How hard should you train](https://fitnessvolt.com/jeff-nippard-how-hard-should-you-train/), [When should you train to failure](https://rosetta.to/u/jeffnippard/when-should-you-train-to-failure-showing-you-our-new-home-in-canada).

## The stretch: where the evidence stands (2023–2026)

- **Nov 2023:** Nippard called long-length partials one of the best-supported intensity techniques; Dr Milo Wolf estimated perhaps 5–10% more growth than full ROM ([BarBend](https://barbend.com/news/long-length-partial-reps/)). Ways to use them: keep going with partials in the stretch after full-ROM failure, or mix "integrated partials" through the set.
- **Sep 2024 / 2025:** his own study with Wolf, Schoenfeld and others in experienced lifters found **no clear extra benefit** of lengthened partials over full ROM ([BarBend](https://barbend.com/?p=289386), [paper](https://peerj.com/articles/18904)).
- **Jul 2025:** dumbbell and cable lateral raises (ROM matched, trained lifters, 8 weeks, sets to failure) grew the side delts by the same amount, about 3–5% ([Larsen, Wolf, Schoenfeld et al. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2025.1611468/full)). The cable's harder-in-the-stretch resistance profile made no detectable difference.
- **Feb 2026:** Jeremy Ethier's co-authored study made one side of the body harder in the stretch and the other harder in the squeeze, across four muscles. Growth was **no different**. Nippard's read in their podcast: the muscles with strong evidence for stretch-focused training are **calves, hamstrings and quads**, and to some extent **biceps** (and overhead work for triceps); for the rest, a full ROM with some tension in the stretch is enough ([podcast](https://pocketcasts.com/podcast/the-jeff-nippard-podcast/bcafed40-0975-0132-a5de-5f4c86fd3263/we-need-to-talk-about-the-stretch-jeremy-ethiers-new-study/976cfce7-2de9-4595-b3e2-326b8ab2f42c), [summary](https://podscan.fm/podcasts/the-jeff-nippard-podcast), [preprint](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/660)).
- **2026 review:** a systematic review of 8 studies (120 participants) concluded long-muscle-length training *may* beat short-length training, including for muscle lengthening, but the evidence base is small ([Sports Med Health Sci 2026](https://www.sciopen.com/local/article_pdf/10.1016/j.smhs.2025.03.001.pdf)).

**Shifu's rule:** always train through a full ROM with tension at the bottom. Choose stretch-biased exercises deliberately for calves (straight-knee raises, bottom-half partials), hamstrings (seated curls), quads (deep squats, leg extension with seat back) and triceps (overhead extensions). Elsewhere, choose the exercise the user can feel, progress and recover from; don't swap a good exercise just because it's "squeeze-biased".

## Low volume while cutting (100-day experiment, Oct 2025)

- He cut to 1–2 hard sets per exercise (about 6 hard sets per muscle per week), taken to or near failure, on an upper/lower split, while dieting.
- Over 100 days DEXA showed about 5.5 lb fat lost and 1.8 lb lean mass lost, with strength PRs on pendulum squat and chest press. He reported enjoying training more.
- He now recommends **lower-volume, high-effort training during a deficit**, because recovery is reduced. This is one person's experiment plus supporting research that **maintaining** muscle needs much less volume than building it.
- Shifu applies it as an option for cuts or busy periods, not a universal rule. Sources: [summary](https://briefread.pages.dev/en/summary/less-work-more-gains-100-day), [video page](https://rosetta.to/u/jeffnippard/i-cut-the-number-of-sets-i-do-in-half).

## Coming back after a break (Jan 2026)

Muscle memory makes regaining muscle faster. Return in phases: lighter loads, less volume, controlled reps, enough protein; build back over a few weeks rather than resuming old numbers. [Source](https://rosetta.to/person/jeff-nippard/2026).

## Warm-up

Raising body temperature is the most important part. Default: about 5–10 min of light cardio, a few dynamic movements for the joints being trained (no long static stretching before lifting), then 2–4 ramp-up sets on the first compound (e.g. ~50% × 5, ~70% × 3, ~85% × 1–2) before working sets. Later exercises for the same muscle need one light feeler set at most. Foam rolling is optional. [Source](https://rosetta.to/u/jeffnippard/warm-up-and-mobility-science-explained-7-studies).

## Planning maths

- **Session length → sets:** a working set plus 1.5–2 min rest takes about 2.5–3 min. After a 10-min warm-up, 60 min holds about 15–20 working sets; 45 min about 12–14. Planning heuristic, not a study finding.
- **Programme length → blocks:** 8 weeks or fewer = one block. Longer = blocks of 4–8 weeks, reassess between. Nippard's own programmes run in roughly 10-week blocks.
- **RIR logging:** "2 RIR" = two more clean reps were possible. Logging RIR lets Shifu check sets are hard enough.
