# Core principles

Master Shifu's defaults come from Jeff Nippard's published framework, checked against the research in [[training-science]]. Where the two disagree, see [[disagreements]].

## The Muscle Ladder (book, Dec 2024)

Nippard's book *The Muscle Ladder: Get Jacked Using Science* (Victory Belt, Dec 2024) organises muscle building into 12 fundamentals: two "rails" and ten "rungs", ordered so the most important come first. [Publisher page](https://penguinrandomhouse.ca/books/717784/the-muscle-ladder-by-jeff-nippard/9781628604863), [book page](https://jeffnippard.com/pages/the-muscle-ladder).

- **Rails (hold everything up):** sustainability and mindset. If a plan can't be kept up, nothing else matters. He has put it bluntly elsewhere: optimal goes out the window if it isn't sustainable ([BarBend](https://barbend.com/?p=206329)).
- **Rungs, in order:** 1 technique, 2 exercise selection, 3 effort, 4 progressive overload, 5 volume, 6 splits and frequency, 7 load and rep ranges, 8 rest periods, 9 advanced techniques, 10 periodisation. Full defaults per rung: [[muscle-ladder]].
- **How Shifu uses it:** fix problems from the bottom rung up. Don't tweak rest times or add drop sets for someone whose technique or effort is off. "The lower rungs aren't sexy but matter most."
- The book also includes 20 programmes for 2 to 6 days a week and photo technique guides for 80+ exercises.

## Five principles for pure muscle growth (video, Oct 2023)

From *How To Train For Pure Muscle Growth* ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=71op1DQ2gyo), [Fitness Volt summary](https://fitnessvolt.com/jeff-nippard-guide-pure-muscle-growth/)). These are the hypertrophy defaults.

1. **Tension drives growth.** Mechanical tension on the target muscle is the main growth signal. Every other rule exists to raise it.
2. **Bodybuilding technique.** Lower under control over about 2–4 s, lift with intent, full range of motion, minimal momentum. If doing partials, do them in the stretched part of the lift.
3. **Train hard enough.** Most people stop too early. Default: 2–3 working sets per exercise; early sets 1–2 reps in reserve (RIR); last set of isolation work to failure. Heavy compounds (squat, bench, deadlift) stop about 2 reps short even on the last set.
4. **Progress every week.** Either add reps at a fixed weight until the top of the range, then add weight (double progression, best for moderate–high reps), or add weight at fixed reps (best for low–moderate reps). Cleaner technique at the same load also counts.
5. **Pick high-tension exercises.** Machines and cables give lots of tension for little fatigue and are safer to take to failure. Compounds alone under-train biceps, side delts, hamstrings and calves, so isolation work is needed.

## How Nippard judges any exercise

Every exercise in his best-and-worst series is scored on three tests. Shifu uses them to judge exercises he never ranked:

1. **Stretch under tension:** the muscle is loaded hard at long muscle lengths.
2. **Feels good:** no joint pain, a smooth resistance curve, a clear mind-muscle connection.
3. **Simple progression:** easy to add a rep or a little load each week.

## Coaching stance

- Evidence first, Nippard second, personal preference third, but adherence beats all three.
- Make small changes and give each one a few weeks before judging.
- Pain is a signal to change the exercise or see a professional, not something to push through.
