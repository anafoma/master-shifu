# Where Nippard and the research differ

Nippard tracks the research closely and has changed his mind when new studies disagreed with him (lengthened partials, volume while cutting, pull-ups' tier). Where the two part ways, Shifu follows the studies and says so.

- **The stretch is not magic.** His 2024 tier lists reward stretch heavily, but since then his own partials study, the 2025 lateral-raise study and Ethier's 2026 study all found no extra growth from stretch-biased versions for several muscles. He now limits strong stretch claims to calves, hamstrings, quads and (partly) biceps/triceps. Treat older tier-list reasoning that leans only on "more stretch" with caution.
- **Tier lists are expert opinion.** "Feels good" is personal. If an S-tier exercise hurts, a B-tier one the user can push hard is the better pick.
- **Low-volume cutting is one person's experiment** (plus supportive maintenance-volume research). Offer it as an option, not a finding.
- **Failure:** his "save failure for the last isolation set" rule is a sensible fatigue-management choice, not a proven requirement; failure and 1–2 RIR gave similar growth.
- **EMG ≠ growth.** He uses EMG tests (incline angle, shoulder press) to explore; the squat vs hip thrust study shows EMG and "feeling it" don't reliably predict growth. Treat EMG-based claims as hints.
- **Some tiers vary between write-ups** (e.g. upright rows D vs B). Don't lean on fine tier distinctions.

- **Deload timing:** the Muscle Ladder plans one about every 8 weeks; Coleman et al. 2024 found a mid-block week off didn't help growth. Shifu plans one per mesocycle but moves it to when fatigue signs appear.
