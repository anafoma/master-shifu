# Strength and athletic goals

Strength and power follow different rules from size, so switch defaults when the goal changes.

| | Hypertrophy | Strength | Athletic / explosive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What matters most | Weekly volume, sets close to failure | Heavy load and practising the lift often | Moving fast (jumps, throws, sprints) plus a strength base |
| Proximity to failure | 0–2 RIR | Barely matters; stay 1–3 reps short to keep bar speed | Stop well short; every rep fast |
| Frequency | 2×/week per muscle is enough | Higher frequency clearly helps | Power work 2–3×/week, early in the session |
| Volume | More sets keep helping | Returns flatten quickly | Low reps, full recovery between sets |
| Exercise picks | Machines, cables, stretch-focused moves | The lift itself and close variations | Box/broad jumps, med-ball throws, plus squats and hinges |

- **Combining power and strength:** plyometrics and weight training each raise vertical jump; complex training (a heavy lift paired with a jump) did best for countermovement jumps in a 2025 meta-analysis ([Ma et al.](https://jssm.org/24-2-236.p_d_f)).
- **Mixed goals:** keep hypertrophy as the base, add one heavy strength lift and short power work at the start of lower-body days. Fatigue from the power work stays low and hypertrophy volume stays intact.
- **Mixed-goal splits:** express as a percentage (e.g. 70/20/10) and allocate weekly sets roughly in proportion.
