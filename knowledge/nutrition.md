# Nutrition basics

Daily totals (protein, energy balance) matter far more than timing. Ana's own nutrition material can extend this file.

## Protein

- Extra protein stopped adding muscle beyond about **1.6 g/kg/day** in a 49-study meta-analysis ([Morton et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/)); the uncertainty is wide, so the working range is **1.6–2.2 g/kg** (0.7–1 g/lb). Nippard uses the same range for bulking and recomposition ([nutrition mistakes](https://rosetta.to/u/jeffnippard/the-worst-nutrition-mistakes-every-lifter-makes), [protein video](https://rosetta.to/u/jeffnippard/the-smartest-way-to-use-protein-to-build-muscle-science-explained)).
- During a deficit, lean towards the top of the range to protect muscle.
- For people with a lot of body fat, about 1 g per cm of height is a simpler target than bodyweight-based numbers.
- Per meal: the body absorbs all of it; the useful amount per meal is likely higher than the old "20–30 g" idea, but daily total matters more. A practical pattern is about 0.4 g/kg per meal across 4+ meals ([Schoenfeld & Aragon 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29497353/)).
- No legitimate safety concern from high protein in healthy people (refer anyone with kidney or other medical conditions to a professional).

## Bulking (Dec 2024 video and related)

- Rate of gain: about **1–2% of bodyweight per month for beginners**, **0.5–1% for intermediate/advanced** ([bulking video](https://rosetta.to/u/jeffnippard/how-to-bulk-like-a-pro-using-science)).
- Surplus: **5–10%** above maintenance for a lean bulk; **10–20%** for faster gains with more fat. Larger surpluses mostly add fat.
- Training drives the muscle gain; the surplus only supports it.

## Cutting (Feb 2026 video)

- Target about **0.5–0.7% of bodyweight per week** (roughly 1 lb/week for many people), about a 500 kcal/day deficit. Bigger deficits increase lean-mass loss ([2026 videos](https://rosetta.to/person/jeff-nippard/2026)).
- Training while cutting: keep effort high; volume can drop (see the low-volume experiment in [[training-science]]).

## Supplements

- **Creatine monohydrate** has the strongest evidence of any muscle-building supplement. Caffeine helps performance. Protein powder is a convenience food.
- Nippard warns people overestimate how much muscle supplements add.

## Hard limits for Shifu

- Never set calorie targets below safe levels, never suggest extreme or crash diets, never give medical advice.
- If a user mentions disordered eating, a medical condition, medication or pregnancy, stop giving numeric nutrition targets and recommend a doctor or registered dietitian.
